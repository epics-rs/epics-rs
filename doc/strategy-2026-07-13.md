# epics-rs strategy — the pivot away from parity-diffing (2026-07-13)

**Status:** adopted. Supersedes the implicit strategy of Rounds 7–19
(`doc/c-parity-review-2026-07-10.md`).

This document records *why* the C-parity audit loop was stopped as a
steady-state practice, what replaces it, and the product policy that
decides the questions the loop kept re-litigating.

---

## 1. The diagnosis: parity-diffing does not converge

Nineteen audit rounds and sixteen fix waves produced 536 filed findings and
483 fix commits. The loop was working — and it was not converging. The
evidence, not the impression:

**Discovery rate never fell.** Unique findings per round:

| R7 | R8 | R9 | R10 | R11 | R12 | R13 | R14 | R15 | R16 | R17 | R18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | 48 | 54 | 42 | 24 | 26 | 32 | 23 | 28 | 25 | 35 | **114** |

Eleven rounds flat in the 22–54 band, then the largest round ever once the
auditor pool was widened. A flat-then-rising curve is the signature of a
process that is **surface-limited, not defect-limited**: we were still
finding new places to look, not running out of defects in the places we had
looked. There is no knee to approach.

**"Clean" was not trustworthy.** Round 18 reopened three entries previously
recorded as *verified clean*, and the inventory carries 21 mentions of
BROKEN / reopened / false-clean verdicts. A coverage process whose clean
verdicts do not hold is not monotone, and a non-monotone process cannot
converge by construction.

**There was no denominator.** ~498k lines of Rust `src` audited by "category"
with a per-round budget. Nobody could say what fraction of the surface had
been swept, so a falling finding count could never be distinguished from
"we looked less this time". The review logs contain six explicit
*did-not-get-to / unaudited / still-owed* admissions.

**The root cause, made concrete.** The record field metadata is *hand-copied*
from C: `crates/epics-base-rs/src/server/records/*.rs` carry **1,174
hand-written `FieldDesc` entries** across 33 files (42 record types), each
transcribing a declaration that already exists, machine-readable, in the
`.dbd`. A large share of the finding stream is exactly this — R18-108 (count
fields declared `LONG`, C says `ULONG`), R18-5 (`HYST` silently dropped),
R18-90 (`SPC_NOMOD` wired to one consumer of two), CBUG-F6 (`SPC_MOD` on
`INPM`..`INPU`). **We had agents proof-reading a thousand hand-transcriptions
by eye, one round at a time.** That has no convergence point, and it is the
correct target for a structural fix rather than more rounds.

## 2. The product policy (decided 2026-07-13)

> **C's bugs are not the contract. Clean is the goal.**

The audit loop kept stalling on a question it had no authority to answer:
when C is wrong, do we reproduce the wrongness? Every CBUG entry required a
case-by-case adjudication, and those adjudications were re-litigated as new
sites were found. That ambiguity is now closed.

**The contract, in three tiers:**

- **Tier 1 — interoperability: mandatory, no deviation.** The CA and PVA wire
  protocols, DBR/DBF type codes, opcode framing, the `.db`/`.dbd` grammar.
  A C client must not be able to tell the difference. This is *protocol*, not
  *implementation*, and it is not negotiable.

- **Tier 2 — semantics: match C where C is right, fix C where C is wrong.**
  Record processing, alarm semantics, monitor posting, link behaviour. C's
  *correct* behaviour is the de-facto spec and we match it. C's *bugs* we do
  **not** reproduce: we implement the intended behaviour, document the
  deviation, and report it upstream. `doc/upstream-c-bugs.md` is the record.

- **Tier 3 — drivers and applications: correctness only, no parity.** asyn
  drivers, areaDetector plugins, motor vendor controllers, optics, scaler,
  modbus, mqtt. Nobody diffs a stepper driver against C — the motor moves or
  it does not. These are tested against hardware and simulators, **not**
  against C source. Auditing them line-by-line against C was the single
  largest misallocation of the previous strategy.

**What this policy immediately changes.** The `NOT-REPRODUCED` bucket in
`doc/upstream-c-bugs.md` becomes the **default**, and the 18 existing
`REPRODUCED` entries become a re-adjudication backlog (§4). It also reverses
a decision merged this week: **R18-6** (`ee4f6bff`) made sCalc's `CRC16`
reproduce C's signed-`char` sign-extension. Under the clean policy that is
wrong — the standard CRC is the one a real Modbus device expects, so C's bug
means C is broken against real hardware for binary payloads. It must be
reversed.

## 3. The replacement: generate from the spec, then diff behaviour

Two mechanisms replace the audit loop. Both convert an unbounded *reading*
problem into a bounded, automatable, regression-proof one.

### 3.1 Generate the field tables from `.dbd` (kills a defect family by construction)

`/home/stevek/work/epics-base/dbd/*.dbd` are the built, macro-expanded,
pod-stripped record declarations — the machine-readable spec, already on
this machine. They carry everything the port hand-copies and more: field
name, `DBF_*` type, `special(...)`, `pp(TRUE)`, `asl(...)`, `size(N)`,
`menu(...)`, `initial(...)`.

Codegen these into the `FieldDesc` tables at build time and the entire
transcription-error family becomes **unrepresentable**, not merely fixed.
This is the project-scale application of the standing doctrine (*structure
over patch; make illegal states impossible by construction*).

**The generator is also an instant, complete audit.** Diffing the generated
tables against the 1,174 hand-written entries yields the *entire*
field-declaration defect set mechanically, in one pass, with no false-cleans
— the same class of finding that took twelve rounds to surface one at a time.

The generator additionally exposes attributes the port's `FieldDesc` does not
model at all today (`pp`, `asl`, `size`, `menu`, `initial`, the `special()`
dispatch code). Each absent attribute is its own finding family; they are
enumerated by the generator rather than discovered by eye.

### 3.2 Differential oracle harness (makes "clean" provable)

C `softIoc` is built at `/home/stevek/work/epics-base/bin/linux-x86_64`.
Boot it and the Rust IOC on the same `.db`, drive both with identical CA/PVA
operation sequences, and diff only what is **observable**: value, DBR type,
`STAT`/`SEVR`, monitor event sequence and count, `NORD`/`NELM`, access
rights.

The `.dbd` supplies the denominator: record types × fields × operations ×
boundary-value classes is finite and enumerable, so **coverage becomes a
percentage** — the thing the audit loop could never report.

**The oracle and the CBUG catalogue compose.** Under the clean policy, a
difference between C and the port is not automatically a port bug: it is
either a port bug, or a *deliberate deviation from a C bug*. The oracle's
expected-deviation allowlist **is** `doc/upstream-c-bugs.md`. Every
NOT-REPRODUCED entry becomes an allowlist row with its justification; any
diff not on the allowlist is a port defect. That makes both artifacts
self-checking: an allowlist row that stops firing means the deviation is gone
(possibly a regression), and a diff that is not on the list is a finding.

Failures shrink to a minimal `.db` + operation sequence and file
automatically. Where the audit loop's green tests proved nothing, an oracle
run is a measurement.

### 3.3 What the 536 findings are for

They are not merely closed — they are **harvested as the oracle's generators**.
The real asset of nineteen rounds is not the fixes; it is the *defect
taxonomy*: the shapes divergence takes in this port (dead subsystems,
two-consumer declarations wired to one consumer, monitor-posting negative
space, boundary-value narrowing). Each taxon becomes a case generator.

## 4. Backlog created by this decision

1. **Re-adjudicate the 18 `REPRODUCED` CBUG entries** under the clean policy.
   Default: implement the intended behaviour, document the deviation, keep the
   upstream report. Includes: `CBUG-A2` (NINT/MODULO `INT_MIN` cast), `A3`
   (`ISINF` signed ±1), `B1`/`B4` (pf4 interpolation, opaque-on-unknown), `B6`
   (flow control cannot be disabled), `B8` (missing IAC stuffing), `B10`
   ("write is not supported" on a read), `B11` (`SoftTrigger 0` triggers),
   `B12` (`ackAt` sentinel), `B13` (motor `CDIR`), `B18`/`B19` (scaler posts),
   `B25` (TimeSeries narrowing), `C2` (circuit reset on parse failure), `D4`
   (escape printers), `E2` (`dbConvert` bare cast), `F8` (CRC16 — **reverse
   `ee4f6bff`**), `F12` (histogram dead alarm — actually raise it).

2. **Two entries need explicit sign-off, because "clean" is ambiguous:**
   - **CBUG-E2 / `types::c_cast`** — out-of-range double→integer. C (x86-64)
     yields `INT_MIN`; wave 15 deliberately reproduced that. Clean could mean
     *saturate* (Rust's natural `as`) or *reject the put with an error*. These
     differ observably on the wire for every numeric field. **Recommend:
     saturate**, and alarm; but this is a Tier-1-adjacent value change and
     should be decided, not assumed.
   - **CBUG-D4** — `\0` vs `\x00` escape rendering is arguably an interop
     format, not a bug. **Recommend: keep C's rendering** (Tier 1) unless it
     provably breaks a consumer.

3. **Round 19 is still in flight** (6 auditor panels). Its findings are
   harvested as oracle generators and as a final manual sweep; no Round 20 is
   scheduled.

4. **Tier 3 crates stop being audited against C source.** They need a
   simulator/hardware test strategy instead — separate document, separate
   effort.

## 5. Exit criteria (what "done" means, now that it can be said)

The previous strategy had no definition of done, which is why it could not
finish. The new one does:

- **Tier 1:** the oracle's protocol suite is green, and its coverage of the
  enumerated wire surface is reported as a percentage.
- **Tier 2:** the oracle's record/field suite is green at a stated coverage
  percentage of the `.dbd`-derived denominator, with every diff either fixed
  or on the CBUG allowlist with a justification.
- **Tier 3:** per-crate, against its own simulator/hardware criteria — never
  against C source.

Release maturity is then stated per crate honestly, rather than gated on a
whole-workspace "no known gaps" claim that no process could substantiate.
